Description

The client eligibility guidelines are intended to assist the caseworker in determining client eligibility for internally and externally delivered programs and/or services.

Overview

The program area has obligations under several signed agreements with the Government of Canada to provide services for the purpose of providing information to help workers insured or not insured or whether they are claiming employment insurance benefits or not, employers, workers' organizations and interested public and private organizations providing employment assistance services to workers.

Manitoba administers services and programming based on the following agreements signed with The Government of Canada:

The Canada-Manitoba Labour Market Development Agreement (signed April 1997, amended May 2009 and December 2016)

The Canada-Manitoba Workforce Development Agreement (signed March 2018)

Guidelines

The program area a provides services to individuals who require assistance in entering or re-entering the labour market or who wish to explore employment related information and options. These services include:

career change guidance

career options

referral to specialized testing services

information on training options/opportunities

Eligibility for general services

All individuals who are authorized to work in Canada are eligible for general services offered at Manitoba Jobs and Skills Development (MJSD) Centres. These include:

Canadian citizens

Immigrants and refugees with permanent resident status, as demonstrated by a Permanent Resident Card from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada. Permanent residents are entitled to remain in Canada on a permanent basis while remaining a citizen of another country (provided certain conditions continue to be met). Permanent residents are entitled to certain rights and privileges in Canada, including the right to apply for Canadian citizenship.

For more information regarding the Permanent Resident Card:

Temporary residents who are not Canadian citizens or permanent residents (e.g., foreign students, individuals on work visas). Temporary residents can be legally authorized to work in Canada. These individuals are issued a temporary Social Insurance Number (SIN) that begins with a “9” and are eligible for general services.

Supplemental information when determining eligibility

The following supplemental information outlines common groups of individuals that may be approaching a MJSD Centre for general services. The information below assists staff in determining eligibility.

(a) Refugee claimants

Refugee claimants who do not have a SIN are not eligible to receive general services but may be able to receive services from various community organizations. Refugee claimants can apply for a work permit and may be authorized to work in Canada. If approved for a work permit a refugee claimant may qualify to be issued a SIN (beginning with a “9”) from Service Canada. Only refugee claimants with a SIN are eligible for general services from a MJSD Centre.

(b) Foreign workers

Foreign workers are eligible to receive general services as they are authorized to work in Canada and have been issued a SIN. An individual who is authorized to work in Canada through Service Canada’s Temporary Foreign Worker Program (TFWP) is issued a temporary time limited SIN that begins with ‘9’.

Due to the nature of the work permit, these individuals are limited in the type of employment that they can seek and are only authorized to work for one designated employer. These authorizations are not transferable to another employer, therefore making their availability to look for work with another employer not possible unless the client contacts Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada and is issued a revised work permit. Foreign workers may be eligible for Special Benefits but due to limited availability based on the limitations of the work permit, will not collect regular EI type II benefits.

(c) Incarcerated individuals

Individuals who are currently incarcerated do not qualify for general services or programs including financial support. These individuals should be encouraged to contact an MJSD Centre upon their release. Individuals who are incarcerated are not available to actively seek employment. Caseworkers are not able to accurately assess a client who is incarcerated, nor will this individual be able to properly do their occupational research or work with a caseworker to create a return-to-work action plan.

(d) SIN beginning with ‘9’

Individuals with a SIN that begins with a “9” are eligible for general services. A SIN is issued by Service Canada to individuals who are neither Canadian citizens nor permanent residents. These SINs are temporary and only valid until the expiry date printed on the front of the card. The expiry date corresponds to the date on the document from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada authorizing that person to work in Canada.

Service Canada grants an implied status of up to eight weeks for individuals who experience an administrative delay after requesting a renewal of their work permit from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada. This implied status is granted only if the renewal application was made prior to the original work permit document expiring. Individuals with an expired SIN who have not requested a renewal of their work authorization before expiration date are not eligible for general services.

Client eligibility for programming

Eligibility for programming may provide individuals financial support while participating in Apprenticeship, Skills Development, Self Employment and Employment Partnerships programs. Wage Subsidy programming for an individual is paid to the contracted employer providing on-the-job training (rural and northern service area only).

Individuals that are eligible for programming meet the eligibility criteria for general services. In addition, individuals need to meet eligibility under the Labour Market Development Agreement (LMDA), or Workforce Development Agreement (WDA) as outlined in the individual program parameters. Supplemental information when determining eligibility for programming

In addition to eligibility outlined in the individual program policies (Apprenticeship, Skills Development, Self Employment, Employment Partnerships and Wage Subsidy), the following outlines common groups of individuals that may approach for financial assistance under LMDA or WDA programming.

(a) Refugee claimants

Refugee claimants without a SIN are not eligible to receive financial support through programming but may be able to receive services from various community organizations. Refugee claimants can apply for a work permit from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada and may be authorized to work in Canada. If approved for a work permit, a refugee claimant may qualify to be issued a SIN beginning with a “9” from Service Canada.

(b) Foreign workers

Foreign workers are not eligible to receive financial support through programming. Foreign workers may be eligible for Special Benefits, but due to limited availability based on the limitations of the work permit, they will not collect Regular Part I EI benefits.

(c) Incarcerated individuals

Individuals who are currently incarcerated do not qualify to receive financial support through programming. Individuals should be encouraged to contact a MJSD Centre upon their release. Caseworkers are not able to assess a client who is incarcerated, nor will the individual be able to properly do their occupational research or work with a caseworker to create a return-to-work action plan.

(d) SIN beginning with ‘9’

Individuals with a SIN that begins with a “9” may be eligible to receive financial support through programming from the program area. Temporary SINs that begin with the number “9” and includes an expiry date.

Individuals with a work permit do not need a study permit if the training course or program is being taken in Canada and the duration is six months or less. The training course or program must be completed within the period authorized to stay in Canada to be considered for programming.

Individuals that are participating in a training course or program that is longer than six months require a study permit administered and issued by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada. A letter of acceptance from the training institution must be issued to the individual. Individuals who obtain an approved study permit that allows them to study may be considered for programming.

LMDA and WDA eligibility criteria for programming

Programming is based upon eligibility set out under the Labour Market Development Agreement (LMDA) and the Workforce Development Agreement (WDA). Eligibility for programming under Apprenticeship, Skills Development, Self Employment and Employment Partnerships are based on these agreements.

Individuals that request assistance for programming are assessed for eligibility based on the eligibility criteria of the LMDA and WDA agreements.

The determining factor for programming eligibility under LMDA is that the individual meets the definition of an EI insured participant, meanwhile for an individual to be eligible for programming under WDA, the client is considered ‘non-EI insured.’

There are two program funding appropriations:

Canada-Manitoba Labour Market Development Agreement (LMDA)

Canada-Manitoba Workforce Development Agreement (WDA)

Each appropriation has a separate set of program policies and client eligibility criteria Refer to program parameters for detailed information on specific programs.

The following describes the two appropriations and those clients who may be eligible to participate in the programming and/or receive direct financial support from the program area.

  1. 1) Labour Market Development Agreement (LMDA) programing

Clients must meet the definition of an insured participant to be eligible for LMDA programs. Eligibility factors:

Insured participants; and

Unemployed and/or working less than an average of 20 hours per week.

Insured participants definition

An insured participant for the purpose of determining EI Part II eligibility is:

Active EI claimant: an unemployed person for whom an EI benefit period is established

Former EI claimant: an unemployed person whose EI benefit period has ended with the previous 60 months

Premiums Paid Eligibility (PPE): An unemployed person who paid EI premiums in at least five of the last ten calendar years that did not entitle the person to a refund (If the person made less than $2000 in earnings in a year, any EI premiums that they would have paid, would be reimbursed)

Note (1): a client who is collecting Band Income Assistance and is insured may also be considered for LMDA provincial benefit programming. Band income assistance is income support administered by First Nations and managed by Crown-Indigenous Relations and Northern Affairs Canada.

Note (2): verification of a clients’ eligibility as an insured participant can be queried through the Service Canada LMDA Access application. Refer to

Unemployed and/or working less than an average of 20 hours/week definition

Under Service Canada interpretation, individuals are considered unemployed if they meet the definition of an insured participant as well as the criteria listed below.

Individuals must be facing barriers to employment and will be considered unemployed and eligible for participation if they:

Are working less than an average of 20 hours per week and are actively seeking full-time employment; or

Are in receipt of a notice of imminent lay-off, or

Must leave their current occupation due to medical reasons, or

Are unemployed, or

An insured participant.

To determine whether individuals have worked less than an average of 20 hours per week, caseworkers should consider the individuals’ hours over at least the previous six months. If they have been working in their current job for less than six months, look at their hours throughout their employment history.

LMDA Program - Employment Assistance Services (EAS)

The program delivers services in the community through contracts with external service providers funded by the LMDA EAS program funds. The funding to community-based organizations delivers a variety of short-term, employment-related services that are tailored for unemployed individuals who require assistance to find a job.

The EAS program is considered a provincial measure that does not require a client to be an insured participant. No direct financial support is provided to clients participating in EAS services.

All Manitobans who are unemployed and/or working less than an average of 20 hours per week and are actively seeking full time employment may participate in EAS programming funded through the LMDA appropriation. This includes individuals collecting Employment and Income Assistance (EIA) and Band Income Assistance

(administered by First Nations and managed by Crown-Indigenous Relations and Northern Affairs Canada).

The individual continues to be eligible if they choose to continue working less than an average of 20 hours per week while participating in an EAS program.

  1. 2) Workforce Development Agreement (WDA) programming

Manitoba has agreed to use the funding provided under the WDA to assist the following individuals:

Unemployed individuals who are not employed, i.e., are not currently working for pay and not self-employed. These individuals are not EI insured clients (non-EI active or Reachback, PPE) including but not limited to:

EIA recipients

Band Income Assistance recipients

Immigrants

Persons with disabilities

Older workers

Youth

Indigenous peoples

New entrants and re-entrants to the labour market

Unemployed individuals previously self-employed; and

Women

employed individuals who are low skilled, specifically, employed individuals who do not have a high school diploma or a recognized certification, or who have low levels of literacy and essential skills.

Employed individuals who are low skilled definition

Individuals who are currently doing any work for pay or profit as an employee for someone else. Also include those who are self-employed, i.e., persons whose sole source of employment income is self-employment. Self-employed persons are working owners of business (incorporated or non-incorporated), farm or professional practice and self-employed workers who do not own their own business (i.e., commission sales, caregiver, newspaper carrier). A self-employed worker acts as a person in business on his/her own account.

To be eligible for WDA programming under the employed individuals who are low skilled beneficiary category, an employed individual must meet at least one of the criteria of low skilled as defined below:

1). Low skilled

Refers to employed individuals who lack knowledge, skills, and/or competencies to keep their employment or advance in their occupation or industry. An individual is considered low skilled when they meet one of the following criteria:

No high school diploma or other recognized certification refers to individuals who do not have documented formal recognition by a governing body (i.e., formal institution, professional body, or sanctioned certifying agency) confirming that they have attained educational and/or occupational proficiency (i.e., certificate, diploma, or degree).

Individuals who have not completed their secondary school education or received a secondary education diploma (K-12 or equivalent, mature diploma). This excludes persons with a post-secondary certificate, diploma, or degree.

Individuals who do not have the certification (secondary education diploma or otherwise) required to remain in their occupation/industry as industry standards change and evolve; and

Individuals who have certification/credentials (i.e., secondary education diploma) from another country, but these credentials are not recognized in Canada.

2). Low levels of literacy

The International Adult Literacy Survey (IALS) defines literacy as “using printed and written information to function in society, to achieve one’s goals, and to develop one’s knowledge and potential.” Literacy refers to a broad range of skills used in a variety of settings (education, home, workplace).

3). Low levels of skill competencies

Refers to individuals who lack one or more of the nine skills for success as defined by industry requirements. Skills include adaptability, collaboration, communication, creativity and innovation, digital, numeracy, problem solving, reading and writing.. For more information:

Note: employed individuals could have some form of certification (i.e., high school diploma, college certificate) and have a high level of literacy but could still lack an essential skill(s), which is preventing or will prevent them from keeping their employment or remaining in their industry or occupation. These individuals are eligible for WDA programming.

Specific client information

Caseworkers should understand the following situations and may wish to discuss these options with their manager:

  1. 1) Skills development

The Skills Development program aligns with changing Manitoba labour market demands. Eligibility criteria for the skill development program, refer to .

  1. 2) Individuals on special benefits

Individuals that are an active EI claimant collecting Special Benefits, i.e., sick leave, maternity or parental benefits, are not eligible for sponsored training due to claim type (Sick Leave/Maternity/Parental). Individuals must convert to Regular Benefits to be eligible for sponsored training while on EI benefits.

Individuals on a Maternity or Parental Benefits who choose not to return to their employers or have quit their jobs and have not applied for Regular EI benefits are considered eligible if they:

Meet the definition of an Insured Participant, or

Eligible for or in receipt EIA

The caseworker should determine why the individual left their employment and should take this into consideration when determining what is appropriate for the client’s employment plan. The caseworker should not deny service to an individual whose maternity/parental claim has exhausted and who is not returning to their last job because they quit. The caseworker completes a thorough needs assessment and determines the most appropriate employment plan with the individual.

  1. 3) Individuals indefinitely disqualified or disentitled from EI benefits

Insured participants where LMDAAccess indicates the claim as active and are indefinitely disqualified or disentitled are not considered priority clients. Although these clients qualify as insured participants (eligible) under the Service Canada criteria, they will not be considered a priority for additional assistance for programs and services, unless the manager approves an exception.

Note: LMDAAccess for Reachback or PPE eligible participants will not display a previous disqualification or disentitlement, and this information is not available from Service Canada. LMDAAccess makes the determination that they meet the definition of an insured participant only.

  1. 4) Individuals with severance pay allocated on EI benefits

Individuals that meet the definition of an insured participant and have received a severance package from their previous employer may not collect EI benefits immediately. The monies received from a severance package are allocated into their claim. Individuals are considered unemployed, without a job to return to and meet the definition of an insured participant, although they may not actively be collecting EI benefits.

5). Retroactive approval for training

Individuals that begin a training course on their own, prior to meeting with a caseworker and without receiving approval of sponsorship are not eligible for funding. The manager may approve the request for retroactive training on an exception-only basis.

An example where retroactive approval may occur:

An individual makes an effort but is unsuccessful to meet and receive approval in anticipation of training due to high client volumes in the MJSD Centre.

An individual in receipt of EIA begins a training program before meeting with and receiving approval from a caseworker.

  1. 6) Overpayments

It is recommended that the program area does not fund anyone who has an outstanding overpayment with the program area. Clients can receive services, but no financial assistance is given until the overpayment has been cleared. The caseworker determining the eligibility should check to see if there is a previous case in the Integrated Case Management (ICM) system. Documentation of overpayment activity occurs in case notes. In exceptional circumstances, the caseworker may discuss options with their manager. The manager will consider additional information and may recommend alternatives.

  1. 7) Funding cap over five year period

Individuals who received training support and completed their training within the past five years (based on the end date of the last training intervention) will only be eligible to receive the balance of funding up to the current funding maximum.

  1. 8) Marketable skills

Support for training will be dependent on the individual demonstrating to the program area that their current qualifications/skills/experience do not make them marketable in their local labour market and require skills training to find employment.

  1. 9) Labour Force Attachment

To be eligible for programming, individuals must meet the following criteria:

Individuals who are EI insured participants as defined in Section 58 of the EI Act.

Individuals must have attachment to the labour force (out of school for two years or more)

With the exception of EIA clients, individuals who are not regular full- time students.

A regular full-time student is an individual who was registered full-time at an educational institution during the last academic year, is intending to return to school in the upcoming academic year, and/or who has not yet made the transition from school to work (i.e., they have not left school to join the labour force on a permanent basis). Full-time as defined by the educational institution. Full-time could be 60% or 100% of a full course load depending on the institution.
